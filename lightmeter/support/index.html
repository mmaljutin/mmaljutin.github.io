Light Meter support

Found a bug, a reading that looks wrong, or a film missing from the list? Write to mmaljutin@gmail.com. Please mention your iPhone model and, if you can, attach a screenshot.

Common questions

How accurate is the meter?
It reads the exposure the iPhone camera chose and the brightness of the zone you meter, then works out the settings for your film. Phones differ a little, so if your negatives come out consistently dense or thin, calibrate it: Settings → Calibrate with a reference meter, or shift it by hand in tenths of a stop.

Spot, Area or Average?
Average meters the whole viewfinder. Spot and Area meter a zone; tap the viewfinder to move it onto the part of the scene that matters, for example a face or the shadows you want to keep.

What is the reciprocity note under the result?
For long exposures many films need more light than the meter says. When you pick a film from the list, the app adds the correction from the maker's data sheet and tells you when the maker has no data that far.

How do I use the journal?
Pick your film with the film button, then tap the camera button for each shot. Frames are numbered on the roll for you; add a note right after the shot. Open a roll in the journal to export it as CSV.

I swap backs or cameras mid-roll.
Give each roll a label such as “Back A”. When you switch, load that roll from the film button or from the journal, and its numbering carries on.

What does Pro add, and is it a subscription?
Pro is a one-time purchase that unlocks the shooting journal: frames with settings saved to Photos, locations and the roll log. The first 10 frames are free. The light meter itself is free and stays free.

I bought Pro and reinstalled the app, or have a new iPhone.
Open Settings → Restore purchase, signed in with the same Apple ID.

The viewfinder is black.
Allow camera access in the iPhone's Settings → Light Meter → Camera.
